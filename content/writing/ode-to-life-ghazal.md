# Ode to Life

*High-school original ghazal (with a short craft reflection) for AP Literature — radif: “forever.”*

“I love that word. Forever. I love that forever doesn't exist, but we have a word for it anyway, and use it all the time. It's beautiful and doomed.”- Viv Albertine

Ode to Life

Hear: the soft song plays in the ears of all, that the phantom sound is clear. Like the golden-crowned
sparrow in the depth of fall, the melody rings in heavenly halls, the song: Forever
And God looked upon us with a pitying glance, for he had forever to spare, while men
toiled through their purposeless endeavor, never aware of his Godly thought: Forever
In our depth of agony, our souls can only sense- the doom of the scent so ominous,
the smell of death is near, his marching song, his mare. He charges on. Is death not Forever?
We lie in the coalbed of destiny, blind idiots we are, ripped into living, teared
apart, glued together, gifted pain and glory, is that the solemn song of Forever?
We collect jubilations to better our existence. We withdraw from pain, perpetual
resistance. Revolutionaries against destiny, and the terrors of Forever.
If my name ‘joy’, meant anything at all, perhaps it will bring me certainty that I shall
live in the tune of the golden-crowned sparrow, not tomorrow, but certainly forever.
Reflection:
The inspiration for the ghazal came from the quote by british singer and writer Viv albertine, which beautifully summarizes the mysterious concept of foreverness in contrast to the very spontaneous and always changing nature of life. It is strange that we often use the word forever without in fact meaning for it to represent what it actually is; it is a concept that we cannot grasp due to the perpetuity of the duration. As such, I chose my radif to be the word “forever”. I intended the poem to be a contemplation of the concept as an almost divine and otherworldly one, a song quite unable to be comprehended, though it is everywhere and in everything. In the maqta, I chose to refer to myself by the meaning of my Chinese name as a way to reference my own identity with the concept of forever. While the thought comes to me, I wave them away so as to live in the moment and not be swept away, ending with the contradicting message that I would live forever in the music of the golden-crowned sparrow as it is my own interpretation of the word forever. The first few couplets mention forever as a godly concept, thereby being capitalized, and when it reaches the last couplet, with my own interpretation, it is no longer capitalized.

Reflection

The premise of the poem was inspired after watching a sad movie and crying a whole lot, when I thought it would be curious to have a conversation with an otherworldly spirit as an in-the-moment writing prompt. The prompt mostly became the first and second couplets. The initial premise of my poem was much shorter in syllables and much more enjambed, so to give myself more space to write, I decided to increase the syllable count to 22 syllables each line, which also gave me more space to play with rhyme and enjambment inside the couplets. As a more stylistic preference, I chose to add rhyme inside the couplets to break up the systematic format of the meter that I tend to see in most ghazals, while still following the basic rules of the poetic form. It is meant as a punctum, as most listeners tend not to find the radif and feel like something is a little off until they hear up to the second or third couplet. The number 22 was also a stylistic choice since the number 2 is often associated with balance and harmony, and to have 22 syllables is meant to hint to the otherworldly nature of the concept of foreverness. It also represents the duality hinted at in the poem by referencing both God and Death as the singers of the song of Forever. There were no major changes between the first draft and the final draft other than to change up a few words here and there so as to make each couplet more distinct and separate ideas. I tend to prefer to start my poems with transition phrases such as “like, and, but”, so to lean closer to the separation of couplets of the ghazal poetry form, I changed a few of those lines to allow them to be more distinct. I believed that I was able to successfully convey what I wished to in the first draft, so no other changes were made.
