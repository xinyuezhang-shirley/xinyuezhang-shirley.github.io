# That flowers fall

*High-school original Shakespearean sonnet (with a short craft reflection) for AP Literature — on mortality, nature, and being alive.*

That flowers fall

Like autumn comes and leaves begin to blush
Like rains confess the wild’s restraining souls
Screams the rowdy youth of such fragile rush
Roars our untamed minds from London to Seoul

Speak love, speak hate and He may laugh at thee
At crazed and blinded fixations like fame
But though He sings slow ballads to the sea
So does He mourn unruly souls like Cain

And look, oh look, that flowers fall as well
and blushing leaves shower idlers in fall
Like wind lies, like rain dies, like waves shall swell
Like flowers fall, like me, all things befall

Does Nature not die certain as shall I?
As Nature is beauty, I am alive.

Reflection

I initially came up with the thematic topic of the poem with the inspiration of Seventeen’s “Fallin’ Flower”, which is a song about the beautiful yet almost melancholic nature of a flower, metaphorically comparing the fall of a flower to falling in love. While I do not speak Japanese, I mostly understood the song through the instrumentals and the melancholic tone, which inspired me to also write a nature based poem. Instead of love, I decided to focus more on the awe of existing, relating to both the falling of flowers and leaves as well as the beauty of the fall season that we are currently experiencing. As an appreciative reader of Shakespearean sonnets, I decided upon writing a Shakespearean sonnet with an iambic pentameter, mostly due to the simple nature of the meter and rhyme that helps reflect the simple yet elegant metaphor that I intended to describe in my poem. Also as a fond reader of classic books, I sprinkled in a few religiously connotated mentions, like the implication that God is looking out for man as much as nature through “mourn souls as unruly as Cain”. This I found to be precisely what was needed to convert the similarity between nature and humans, because it helped convey that since God created us all, we are all valuable and fragile creations by the same artist. Additionally, I found that old English was a great way to cut back on the number of syllables as well as rhyming needed to convey the same message, alas the “laugh at thee” and “Does nature not die certain as shall I?”. While my first draft did take a couple trials, most of them are changes with wording so as to more clearly describe how similar nature is to humans. Initial trials took a more contemplative approach towards nature and tying it to both religion and prayers, but I later found that this approach took away from the intimacy of the comparison between humans and nature as it would simultaneously require humans to be described in a similarly mystical way. Throughout the process, the most trouble that I had was with the last two lines after the volta, since the focus was really on concluding the comparison and bringing the thought back to myself. I found that 20 syllables was not quite enough to put all that I wanted to conclude with the metaphor between humans and nature, so it took quite a bit of rewording and cutting to result in what is now the last two lines of the poem. In the end, I decided to pay yet another homage to Shakespeare to take his idea for a rhetorical question, because I found that it better establishes the contemplative theme of the poem, and helps lead to the last conclusion that compares myself specifically to nature. My revised sonnet is not really different from my first draft because I found that my draft does successfully convey the message that I wished for it to convey. A few minor edits were made to make sure that the syllabic count is the same for each line and the necessary rewording was completed in order for that to be achieved.
